# Azevedo_etal_2009_Table1

Table 1 | Observed and Expected Cellular Composition of the Human Brain According to the Cellular Scaling Rules for Primate Brains. Source: `Azevedo-2009-Equal numbers of neu.pdf`, PDF page 8 (journal page 539), DOI in `__ReadMe.xlsx`.

## Build

Table 1 prints five blocks. Each block conditions on one observed quantity (a primate of 75 kg; a primate brain of 1,508 g; a primate cortex of 1,233 g; a primate cerebellum of 154 g; a primate RoB of 118 g) and gives, per quantity, the value **Expected** under the primate cellular scaling rules of Herculano-Houzel et al. (2007), the **Observed** value, and the percentage **Difference**. Only the Observed column is this paper's data, and only it is built: the Expected and Difference columns remain in the snapshot and never enter the analysis CSV or the public TSV. Counts are printed in billions (the table's footnote: "Results are given in billions") and are converted to absolute numbers; masses are in grams. The result is a single row for *Homo sapiens* (four pooled male donors, aged 50, 51, 54 and 71 years — `Number` is from the paper's Methods and Results, not from Table 1).

From the paper folder, run:

```sh
Rscript Azevedo_etal_2009_Table1_snapshot.R
Rscript Azevedo_etal_2009_Table1.R
```

The snapshot script captures the table programmatically from the publisher PDF in this folder (snapshot HOWTO method 3: `pdftotext -bbox` word geometry, words clustered into the printed rows and columns, every row anchored on its printed label; no value is typed by hand) and refuses to overwrite a differing frozen snapshot. It was run once for this build. On subsequent runs, run only `Rscript Azevedo_etal_2009_Table1.R`. There is no `fulltext.html`: the article is in neither PubMed Central nor Europe PMC, so no open-access HTML full text exists to freeze; the publisher PDF already in this folder is the frozen source. The R script reads only its item snapshot for measurements and writes `Azevedo_etal_2009_Table1.csv` and the DOI-coded public TSV. The item definitions are in `reference_tables/`.

## Interpretation and checks

- **Observed only.** The Expected columns are predictions from another paper's scaling rules, and the expected values do not even add up among themselves (expected neurons + nonneurons for a 75 kg primate = 172.36 billion vs the 170.97 billion expected cells printed in the same block), because each comes from its own fitted equation. Treating them as data would import Herculano-Houzel et al. (2007)'s model into Azevedo et al.'s measurements. In the build script the Difference column serves only as a capture check: it must recompute from the printed Expected and Observed values (it does, within 0.06 percentage points on all 12 rows; the two loosest cells — whole-brain nonneurons −10.2% and cerebellar nonneurons +42.4% — are consistent with the authors computing from unrounded values).
- **The PDF's text layer corrupts the signs.** The printed "+" of the Difference column is encoded as U+2D19 and the printed minus as U+2013 (the same +/- corruption the HerculanoHouzel_etal_2011 build notes for this publisher). The snapshot maps the two codepoints back to the glyphs actually printed ("+" and "−"); digits and text are taken over unchanged.
- **No SDs.** Table 1 prints none, so every SD column of the analysis CSV is empty. The paper does report mean ± SD in its Results text and figures, and Herculano-Houzel et al. (2015) re-report those SDs against this source; they are not part of this table and were not imported into this build.
- **No body mass.** The "75 kg" of the first block heading is the reference body mass its Expected values are computed for, not a measurement of the donors, so `Body mass, g` is left empty.
- **Structure masses come from the block headings** (1,233 g cortex, 154 g cerebellum, 118 g RoB), rounded to the gram as printed. Their sum, 1,505 g, falls 3 g short of the printed whole-brain mass of 1,508 g; the unrounded masses in Herculano-Houzel et al. (2015) (1,232.93 + 154.02 + 117.66 = 1,504.61 g vs a whole-brain 1,508.91 g) show the same gap, so it is a property of the measurements, not of the rounding alone. Kept as printed.
- **Internal sums.** The observed structure values sum exactly to the printed whole-brain values for neurons (16.34 + 69.03 + 0.69 = 86.06 billion) and for nonneurons (60.84 + 16.04 + 7.73 = 84.61 billion). The printed whole-brain total of 170.68 billion cells exceeds the sum of its own printed parts (170.67 billion) by 0.01 billion — rounding in the printed table; the build script asserts the sums with that tolerance and keeps every value as printed. (The Results text's cerebellar total, 85.08 billion cells, is likewise 0.01 above the table's summed 85.07.)
- **Cross-checks.** Every observed value agrees with the paper's Results text (brain mass 1,508.91 ± 299.14 g; 170.68 ± 13.86 billion cells; 86.06 ± 8.12 billion neurons; cortex 16.34 ± 2.17 billion neurons and 60.84 ± 7.02 billion nonneurons; cerebellum 69.03 ± 6.65 and 16.04 ± 2.17 billion; RoB 7.73 ± 1.45 billion nonneurons) and with the *Homo sapiens* rows Herculano-Houzel et al. (2015) cite to this paper in their Tables 1, 2, 3 and 5. Derived ratios of the printed means reproduce the 2015 compilation's printed ratios exactly (cortical O/N 3.723, cerebellar O/N 0.232, RoB O/N 11.203); its printed densities differ from ratios of these means because they average per-individual densities instead.
- The snapshot was captured programmatically and checked by the assistant against the rendered PDF page, the paper's text, and the 2015 re-report; human visual signoff remains pending in the registry. These are source measurements, not a normal-versus-condition contrast. No merge is performed by this build.
