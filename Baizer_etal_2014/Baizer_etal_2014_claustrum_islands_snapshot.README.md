# Baizer et al. (2014): qualitative dorsal-claustrum observations

Source: Baizer, J. S., Sherwood, C. C., Noonan, M., & Hof, P. R. (2014). Comparative organization of the claustrum: what does structure tell us about function? *Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience*, 8, 117. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2014.00117

Local source: [`baizer_etal_2014.pdf`](baizer_etal_2014.pdf). Companion table: `Baizer_etal_2014_claustrum_islands_snapshot.csv`.

## Scope

This is a source-faithful, manually assembled snapshot of the paper's explicit observations of separated dorsal-claustrum cell clusters in four mammalian taxa. Each row cites the relevant figure or text and preserves the authors' qualitative extent descriptions. `yes` means separated clusters are reported or illustrated; it does **not** encode a standardized severity score. Blank cells mean the source does not give that value, not absence or zero.

The macaque is identified only as a “macaque monkey” in this paper, so no species name has been assigned. The two gorilla brains are described in Materials and Methods, but the pictured sections and reported dimensions are not attributed to a particular individual. The 33 mm rostrocaudal and up-to-20 mm dorsoventral measurements refer to the gorilla claustrum as a whole, not to a cell island. The dolphin and whale observations use archival tissue photographed for this paper.

## Boundaries and overlap

- Figure 5 redraws eight species from Kowiański et al. (1999). It is excluded here because it is reused evidence already represented by the Kowiański source in Evo-M1-Trait-Data.
- Figures 1, 2, 4 and 6 illustrate cat, macaque and human anatomy, including earlier archival/immunostaining work. They do not provide a comparable count of separated clusters across species. No unreported negative states are coded.
- The paper provides no tabulated island counts, comparable claustrum volumes, or cross-species neuron size/density values. It explicitly cautions against comparing neuronal size or packing density between cat/monkey and gorilla because tissue preparation differed (PDF p. 6).
- The observations are suitable as documented qualitative evidence. Any harmonized morphological character or ordinal fragmentation score would require a separate coding protocol and review of the figures.

## Comparative character

`island_pattern_class` is an analytical coding of the authors' explicit comparison in the abstract and conclusion: `few_local_clusters` for the macaque and gorilla, and `many_dispersed_islands` for the dolphin and humpback whale. The two classes distinguish the pattern reported for these four taxa; they are **not** numerical island counts or evidence of absence in any other species. The original qualitative phrases and anatomical locations remain alongside the class.

A second, narrower character is in `Baizer_etal_2014_dorsal_enlargement_snapshot.csv`: the reported pole of the enlargement in cat versus macaque. These observations use archival material and are not new measurements from this paper.
