# Charvet et al. — Table 1: specimens and stereology setup

Charvet, C. J., Cahalane, D. J., & Finlay, B. L. *Systematic, cross-cortex variation in neuron numbers in rodents and primates*. DOI [10.1093/cercor/bht214](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bht214). This paper appeared online in 2013 and in *Cerebral Cortex* 25(1):147–160 in 2015. The repository follows the online year in `Charvet_etal_2013`. Source: `Charvet-2013-Systematic, Cross-Co.pdf`, PDF page 2, printed Table 1.

An AI assistant transcribed the table on 2026-11-24 into `Charvet_etal_2013_Table1_snapshot.csv` and checked the entries against the rendered PDF page. The snapshot retains the source orientation: measures as rows, six specimen columns, the truncated printed caption, the hamster brain-weight superscript `a`, and its Chalfin et al. (2007) footnote. It is frozen; the R reader does not rebuild it.

Run `Rscript --vanilla Charvet_etal_2013/Charvet_etal_2013_Table1.R` from the repository root. The reader writes the six-row analysis CSV and DOI-coded public TSV, looking up its code by item name in `__ReadMeAddON.xlsx`. It parses correction-factor ranges and mean (SD) into separate fields, converts brain weight from g to project mg, and keeps Table 1's common-name labels. The hamster has no specimen ID (`N/A` in the source) and its brain weight is attributed to Chalfin et al. (2007); these fields are marked accordingly. Sampled neuron counts are counts examined, not whole-isocortex estimates. Definitions and a separate join-ready list of scientific names stated elsewhere in the paper are in `reference_tables/`.

The Methods call *Cebus apella* a “black howler monkey,” while Table 1 and the abstract call it a capuchin. The Methods also spell the owl monkey *Aotus trivirgatus*, while the abstract prints `trivigratus`. The analysis retains the table labels; the separate scientific-name resource records these internal conflicts without asserting a correction.
