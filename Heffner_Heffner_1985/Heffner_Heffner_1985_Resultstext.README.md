# Heffner_Heffner_1985_Resultstext

## Source
Heffner, R. S., & Heffner, H. E. (1985). Hearing in Mammals: The Least
Weasel. *J Mammal*, 66, 745-755. doi:10.2307/1380801

Registry Item **Results text**, printed pp. 745 (Abstract) and 749
(Results, subsections "Best frequency.-", "Best sensitivity.-",
"High-frequency sensitivity.-", "Low-frequency sensitivity.-"). Public
copy: `Heffner_Heffner_1985/Heffner_Heffner_1985.pdf`.

## Why this item exists
This paper reports the first behavioral audiogram for a mustelid
carnivore, the least weasel (*Mustela nivalis*), tested to broaden the
sample of small carnivores available for comparative hearing analysis. The
paper's own summary sentences in the Abstract and in the labeled Results
subsections state the four headline numbers directly (60-dB-SPL hearing
range, region of best hearing, best frequency, best sensitivity), so no
digitisation of Figure 3 (the individual audiogram curves, registered
separately as a lower-priority candidate row) was needed.

## Data role
Primary -- this is the sole species in the paper and the only row.

## Observation level
Species-mean values averaged across two least weasels (individuals "A"
and "B" in Fig. 3), as reported in the Results narrative.

## Units
Low-frequency hearing limit converted from Hz (as printed, 51 Hz) to kHz
(0.051 kHz) for consistency with the high-frequency limit column and the
rest of this project's sensory-trait units; high-frequency limit, region
of best hearing, and best frequency in kHz; best sensitivity in dB SPL
(re 20 uN/m^2 per the Fig. 3 y-axis label).

## Verification note (scanned PDF)
This source is an older scanned/OCR'd article. All six digits used here
(51 Hz, 60.5 kHz, 1 kHz, 16 kHz, 2 kHz, -8 dB) were independently verified
against 150-dpi page renders of the original printed pages (p. 745 and
p. 749) rather than trusted from the raw OCR text layer alone, which shows
scattered character-recognition artifacts elsewhere in the document (e.g.
"chdd" for "Child", "Unive1'stty" for "University"). All values matched
the text layer exactly on visual inspection; no discrepancy found.
