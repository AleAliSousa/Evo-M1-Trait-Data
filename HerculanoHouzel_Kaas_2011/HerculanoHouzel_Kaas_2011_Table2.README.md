# HerculanoHouzel_Kaas_2011_Table2

Table 2 | Deviation from expected cellular composition of the cerebellum. Source: `Herculano-Houzel-2011-Gorilla and oranguta.pdf`, PDF page 4 (journal page 36), DOI in `__ReadMe.xlsx`.

## Build

Table 2 lists nine primate species. Each row label gives the species and its cerebellar mass; the columns then give, under two fitted scaling rules (primate: C<sub>Cb</sub> = 619,105,857.039 × M<sub>Cb</sub><sup>0.987</sup>; rodent: C<sub>Cb</sub> = 386,335,032 × M<sub>Cb</sub><sup>0.766</sup>), the number of cerebellar cells **Expected** from the cerebellar mass, the **Observed** total number of cells, and the percentage **Deviation** (footnote a: Deviation = 100 × (observed − expected)/expected). Only the Observed column is data, and only it — with the cerebellar masses from the row labels — is built: the Expected and Deviation columns are outputs of the fitted equations and remain in the snapshot only. The Observed column is printed twice, identically, once under each rule; the build asserts the two printings agree and uses the value once. Counts are printed in millions or billions and are converted to absolute numbers; masses are in grams.

From the paper folder, run:

```sh
Rscript HerculanoHouzel_Kaas_2011_Table2_snapshot.R
Rscript HerculanoHouzel_Kaas_2011_Table2.R
```

The snapshot script captures the table from the frozen PubMed Central full text in this folder (`fulltext.html`, PMC3064932; snapshot HOWTO method 2: the publisher's own HTML rendering, superscripts caret-rendered, subscripts as plain letters) and refuses to overwrite a differing frozen snapshot. It was run once for this build. On subsequent runs, run only `Rscript HerculanoHouzel_Kaas_2011_Table2.R`. The R script reads only its item snapshot for measurements and writes `HerculanoHouzel_Kaas_2011_Table2.csv` and the DOI-coded public TSV. The item definitions are in `reference_tables/`.

## Interpretation and checks

- **Observed only.** The Expected columns are predictions from the fitted primate and rodent scaling equations (the paper's Table 1 family of equations applied to the cerebellum), and the Deviation columns are arithmetic on those predictions. Neither is a measurement; neither enters the analysis CSV or the public TSV. In the build script they serve only as checks on the capture (below). Tables 1 and 3 of the paper (fitted scaling equations) and Table 4 (model predictions anchored on literature values) are not registered and are not built, following the workbook's practice for equation and prediction tables.
- **Provenance is per row** (the `Cerebellum Source` column). Only two rows are this paper's own measurements: *Gorilla gorilla* (one animal) and *Pongo pygmaeus* (three animals) — whole cerebella donated by the Yerkes Regional Research Center, stored in 4% paraformaldehyde for over 10 years and processed in 2008, one cerebellar hemisphere counted with the isotropic fractionator and doubled. The *Homo sapiens* row is compiled from Azevedo et al. (2009): its mass, 154.02 g, is that paper's unrounded cerebellar mass, and its observed 85.08 billion cells is that paper's Results-text total (Azevedo's Table 1 parts sum to 85.07 billion — a 0.01 billion rounding difference in the source, kept as printed here). The other six rows are compiled from Herculano-Houzel et al. (2007) — "the other primate cerebella analyzed previously" — and Herculano-Houzel et al. (2015) cite the same sources for the same rows. Presenting any of the seven compiled rows as new primary data would double-count the source papers at the wiring step; the Source column is the guard.
- **The primate Deviation percentages of the Pongo and Gorilla rows are transposed in print.** Pongo's row prints 31.9% where its own printed expected and observed values give +37.7%; Gorilla's prints 37.7% where its own give +31.9% — each printed value matches the other row's recomputed deviation (31.92 and 37.75 from the printed numbers). The rodent-block deviations of both rows recompute correctly, the expected values recompute from the printed equations row by row, and the observed values are confirmed by the text and by the 2015 compilation, so the slip is confined to those two percentages, which are not built. The build script asserts the transposition explicitly. The error is in both the publisher PDF and the PMC rendering.
- **Capture checks.** Every printed Expected value recomputes from its group-heading equation at the printed mass within 0.34% (the loosest cell, *Otolemur* in the primate block, reflects its mass being printed rounded to 1.20 g; the unrounded 1.196 g of the 2015 compilation removes the gap). Every printed Deviation recomputes from the printed Expected and Observed within 1.0 percentage point (loosest: *Aotus*, primate block, 0.52 points, from the three-significant-figure printings) apart from the transposed pair above.
- **Cross-checks.** The snapshot (from PMC) was checked against the publisher PDF in this folder: its text layer and a visual read of the rendered page agree with the PMC rendering on all 54 value cells, all 9 species labels and all headings. Against Herculano-Houzel et al. (2015) Table 2, whose *Gorilla* and *Pongo* cerebellum rows cite this paper: the masses agree exactly (37.56 g; 35.06 g) and the neuron + non-neuron sums reproduce this table's observed totals exactly (26.4 + 2.9 = 29.3 billion; 26.3 + 2.2 = 28.5 billion). The same sums for the six 2007 rows and the human row agree with this table's observed values at their printed precision (human: 85.07 vs the printed 85.08 billion, the Azevedo rounding noted above). Note for the wiring step: the 2015 neuron/non-neuron splits for the two apes do not come from this table (which prints totals only) but from this paper's text, where the neuron numbers are granule-cell counts estimated from nuclear morphology — the tissue had been fixed too long for NeuN immunocytochemistry — plus the remainder as non-neurons.
- **No SDs.** Table 2 prints none, so every SD column of the analysis CSV is empty. The paper's text does report SDs for the orangutan (mass 35.06 ± 4.34 g; cells 28.5 ± 3.78 billion); they are not part of this table and were not imported into this build.
- **Number** is filled only where the sample behind the values is stated in this paper (gorilla 1, orangutans 3, Methods) or is the sample of the row's own source paper (human 4, Azevedo et al. 2009). It is left empty for the six rows compiled from Herculano-Houzel et al. (2007), whose sample sizes Table 2 does not print.
- **Fixation caveat (the paper's own).** The ape cerebella spent over 10 years in paraformaldehyde; the authors attribute much of the apes' 30–40% positive deviation from the primate rules to shrinkage of the tissues, which decreases structure mass without affecting the total number of cells. The masses are kept as printed; the caveat is recorded in the definitions file.
- The snapshot was captured programmatically and checked by the assistant against the rendered PDF page, the paper's text, and the 2015 re-report; human visual signoff remains pending in the registry. These are source measurements, not a normal-versus-condition contrast. No merge is performed by this build.
