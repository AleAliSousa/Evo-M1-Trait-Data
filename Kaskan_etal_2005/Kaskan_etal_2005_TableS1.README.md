# Kaskan et al. 2005 — Electronic Appendix A, Table 1 (species and cortical areas)

Kaskan PM, Franco ECS, Yamada ES, Silveira LCL, Darlington RB, Finlay BL (2005). *Peripheral
variability and central constancy in mammalian visual system evolution.* Proc R Soc Lond B
272:91–100. doi:10.1098/rspb.2004.2925.

## What this covers

The full 30-species roster used in this paper's cortical-area analyses, with each species' printed
cortical-area **names** (not sizes) for 12 clustered functional categories (V1, V2, other visual,
A1, R, other auditory, 3b/SI, SII, other somatosensory, M, other motor), and the superscript
reference key to the original mapping study for each species. This is the species list underlying
Figures 2 and 3's cortical-area-scaling analyses — see `Kaskan_etal_2005_Figure3_digitized.csv`
in this folder for the (approximate, digitized) scatter-plot data itself, and the note below on
why Figures 2/3 could not be built from a printed data table.

## Source → snapshot → CSV

- **Source:** `pb050091supp.pdf` (Electronic Appendix A), in this folder — a born-digital PDF; the
  table is printed in landscape/rotated orientation. Every cell was cross-checked against a 350 dpi
  page render (page 2 of 3), rotated and cropped to sub-regions for full legibility, including
  small superscript reference letters and multi-line cells.
- **Snapshot:** `Kaskan_etal_2005_TableS1_snapshot.csv` — journal-faithful layout, including the
  full reference-letter key from the table header.
- **Analysis CSV:** `Kaskan_etal_2005_TableS1.csv` — same content with clean machine-readable
  headers.

## Important limitation

**This table lists which cortical areas were identified and what their local/author-given names
were — it does not give surface-area sizes.** No mm² values for individual cortical areas appear
anywhere in this paper or its supplement outside of Electronic Appendix B (which covers retina
only, not neocortex — see `Kaskan_etal_2005_TableS2`). The actual surface-area measurements
underlying Figures 2 and 3's log-log scaling plots come from the primary mapping studies cited by
the superscript letters in this table (e.g. Krubitzer & Kaas 1993, Krubitzer et al. 1995, etc.) —
none of which are held in this repository. This table is nonetheless useful on its own as a
categorical/nomenclature dataset and as the definitive species roster (30 species = 20 nocturnal +
8 diurnal + 2 monotremes, matching the counts given in the Figure 3 caption) for cross-referencing
against the digitized Figure 3 points.
