## Why this item exists
This paper measured the big brown bat's minimum audible angle (MAA) for
passive sound localization, tested its ability to use binaural time- and
intensity-difference cues, and mapped its retinal ganglion-cell density to
estimate the width of its field of best vision -- all reported in the
Results text. The width of the field of best vision (139 deg) is the
single value SensoryData_compiled currently draws from this paper; the MAA
and binaural-cue findings are retained here for completeness since they
are part of the same Results narrative and are registered together as one
"Results text" item.

## Data role
`field_of_best_vision_width_deg` (139 deg) is the value used by
SensoryData_compiled (`primary`). `sound_localization_threshold_deg`
(MAA, 14 deg average) and the two binaural-cue-use flags are printed in
the same Results section but are not currently drawn on downstream;
they are retained as contextual/secondary within this single-row item.

## Observation level
- MAA: mean of 3 individual bats (16, 12, 13 deg for bats A, B, C
  respectively), overall average 14 deg, as printed.
- Binaural cue use: categorical finding from a 2-animal pure-tone
  localization test -- the bats could use binaural intensity differences
  but not binaural time differences.
- Field of best vision: 139 deg, derived from a retinal ganglion-cell
  density map (isodensity contour at >=75% of maximum), described in the
  same Results section (Sec. 3.2, "Retinal ganglion-cell density").

## Units
MAA in degrees; field of best vision width in degrees of the horizon;
binaural cue use recorded as Y/N.
