# Kowiański et al. (1999), Figure 7: claustrum morphology type

Source: Kowiański, P., Dziewiątkowski, J., Kowiańska, J., & Moryś, J. (1999). Comparative anatomy of the claustrum in selected species: a morphometric analysis. *Brain, Behavior and Evolution*, 53, 44–54.

This snapshot records the authors' **five nominal morphology types** for their nine sampled mammalian taxa. The source is the discussion on printed p. 49 (PDF p. 6), checked against Figure 7 on printed p. 52 (PDF p. 9). It adds a categorical character absent from the existing Table 1 and Table 2 CSVs. `table1_source_row` aligns the nine taxa with the already extracted numerical tables; it does not create independent specimens.

| Type | Source description | Sampled taxa |
|---|---|---|
| I | Poorly separated from cortex; external and extreme capsules poorly developed or absent | Sorex, mouse, rat |
| II | Distinctly separated by better-developed capsules; relatively larger vertical dimension | Guinea pig, rabbit |
| III | Characteristic triangular dorsal claustrum, narrowing ventrally | Cat |
| IV | Thin, vertically oriented ribbon of gray matter | Macaque, Cercopithecus |
| V | Most differentiated human form, with described dorsal, orbital, temporal and paraamygdalar parts | Man |

The Roman labels are **categories, not numeric ranks**. Although the authors discuss structural complexity, the type numbers should not be treated as equally spaced or used as a continuous score. The source mentions hedgehogs and dogs when discussing related literature; neither was in its nine-species sample, so neither is added. Common-name rows stay as published rather than being assigned a species that the methods do not identify. The three explicit scientific names in Methods are preserved separately.

Baizer et al. (2014) reproduce these shapes in their Figure 5. The data belong to Kowiański et al. (1999), not to a second independent Baizer sample. This is a snapshot only; no registry or merge has been changed.
