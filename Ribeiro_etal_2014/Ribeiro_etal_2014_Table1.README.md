# Ribeiro_etal_2014_Table1

Table 1 | Cellular composition of the olfactory bulb. Source: `Ribeiro-2014-Greater addition of neurons to th.pdf`, PDF page 4, DOI in `__ReadMe.xlsx`.

## Build

The printed table lists species as rows under five clade headings — GLIRES (9 species), PRIMATES (5), SCANDENTIA (1), EULIPOTYPHLA (5), AFROTHERIA (3) — and the snapshot keeps that layout, headings included; the build emits one row per species. For each species the table prints olfactory-bulb mass (g), number of neurons (in millions, converted to absolute counts), % neurons and neuronal density (neurons/mg), as species means ± SD. All values refer to **both olfactory bulbs combined** (table footnote: "All values refer to both olfactory bulbs. % Neurons, percentage of cells in the olfactory bulb that are NeuN+. Neurons/mg, density of neurons per miligram of olfactory bulb tissue. Data from Sarko et al. (2009), Herculano-Houzel et al. (2011), Neves et al. (2014) and this study."). The table prints no total cell numbers, no non-neuronal cell counts and no body or brain masses, so the analysis CSV has none. Rows without SDs are exactly the four single-animal species (*Proechimys cayennensis*, *Oryctolagus cuniculus*, *Macaca mulatta*, *Procavia capensis*), whose bulbs were pooled. The cerebral-cortex numbers the paper's analyses compare against are not in this table — the Methods take them from the earlier studies — and are not built here. Nothing in Table 1 is a fitted or predicted value.

**Provenance is per-row, and the analysis CSV carries it in its `Olfactory bulb Source` column.** Table 1 is a compilation as well as a primary source. Measured for this study and first published here (8 species): the five primates (*Microcebus murinus*, *Callithrix jacchus*, *Otolemur garnettii*, *Aotus trivirgatus*, *Macaca mulatta*), *Tupaia glis*, and new, larger samples of *Rattus norvegicus* (5 animals; the 2011 paper had 4, with different values) and *Sciurus carolinensis* (9 animals; 2011 had 3, with different values). Re-reported from Herculano-Houzel et al. (2011) (7 glires): *Mus musculus*, *Mesocricetus auratus*, *Proechimys cayennensis*, *Cavia porcellus*, *Oryctolagus cuniculus*, *Dasyprocta prymnolopha*, *Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris* — mass and neuron values identical to the 2011 table at printed precision. Re-reported from Sarko et al. (2009) (5 eulipotyphlans): species averages, per the Methods. Re-reported from Neves et al. (2014) (3 afrotherians): means identical, SDs restated (see checks). The `Number` column counts **animals** behind each row's values; bulb counts are in the definitions file. The naked mole-rat (*Heterocephalus glaber*) was collected for this study (3 animals, 3 bulbs) but does not appear in Table 1; its bulb data remain only in Herculano-Houzel et al. (2011).

From the paper folder, run:

```sh
Rscript Ribeiro_etal_2014_Table1_snapshot.R
Rscript Ribeiro_etal_2014_Table1.R
```

The snapshot script captures the table from the frozen PMC full text (`fulltext.html`) and refuses to overwrite a differing frozen snapshot. It was run once for this build. On subsequent runs, run only `Rscript Ribeiro_etal_2014_Table1.R`. The R script reads only its item snapshot for measurements and writes `Ribeiro_etal_2014_Table1.csv` and the DOI-coded public TSV. The item definitions are in `reference_tables/`.

## Interpretation and checks

- The snapshot was cross-checked cell-for-cell against the publisher PDF (text layer and a visual read of the rendered page, including a 300 dpi read of the Neurons column): all 92 value cells, 23 species names, 5 clade headings and 4 column headings agree. The one rendering convention is documented in the snapshot script: superscripts are caret-rendered ("10^6", the Gabi et al. (2010) pattern).
- **Printed anomaly kept verbatim in the snapshot**: in all 17 SD-bearing Neurons cells, the SD term is typeset with a plus-minus glyph where a multiplication sign belongs — "3.89 × 10⁶ ± 1.25 ± 10⁶" — in both the PDF and PMC (verified on the rendered page: the glyph is a true ±, not a ×). The build reads the SD as × 10⁶, guarded on the exact printed pattern.
- Derived quantities: the printed densities are means of the individual densities; recomputed as neurons ÷ mass from the printed means they deviate by a median of 1.3% (loosest: *Callithrix jacchus*, 13.5%, whose printed mass SD of 0.014 g exceeds its mean of 0.008 g; *Dasyprocta prymnolopha*, 10.4%; *Mus musculus*, 7.9%). The % neurons of the seven re-reported glires, recomputed from the 2011 counts of neurons and other cells, agree with the printed percentages within 1.1 points.
- Herculano-Houzel et al. (2015), Table 4, cite Ribeiro et al. (2014) for exactly five species rows — the primates. Of their 30 paired cells, 29 agree with this build at printed precision; the exception is the *Otolemur garnettii* neuronal-density SD: 47,590 in 2015 against 43,590 printed here, a probable digit slip on the 2015 side (their *Microcebus* neuron SD, 119,100 against the coarse printed 0.12 × 10⁶, is within that value's rounding interval). Their remaining rows for this table's species cite the original papers — but three of them carry **this** paper's values and sample sizes under other citations: *Rattus norvegicus* (n = 5, cited to Herculano-Houzel et al. (2006), which reports no bulb data), *Sciurus carolinensis* (n = 9, cited to the 2011 paper, whose squirrel sample was 3 animals with different values) and *Tupaia glis* (n = 16 bulbs, cited to Herculano-Houzel et al. (2007)). Their n column also mixes conventions: bulbs for the Ribeiro-cited rows, animals for the Sarko and Neves rows. Which values the merge carries is a wiring decision, not a build decision.
- Against the source papers: the five eulipotyphlan rows agree with Sarko et al. (2009), Table 2, on all 40 paired cells at this table's rounding (their densities for *Sorex fumeus* and *Blarina brevicauda* differ from Sarko's printed densities by 4 neurons/mg — recomputed from the shared raw data, not copied). The three afrotherian rows agree with Neves et al. (2014) on every mean except the *Procavia capensis* bulb mass — 0.286 g here and in Herculano-Houzel et al. (2015), 0.290 g in Neves et al., whose own printed count and density imply 0.286 g — but **every SD printed here for the two n = 2 afrotherians is exactly √2 times Neves et al.'s printed SD** (density SDs 5,708 vs 4,037 and 3,084 vs 2,181; likewise mass and neuron SDs), an SD/SEM-type reporting difference between the two 2014 papers. Herculano-Houzel et al. (2015) carry the larger (Ribeiro) SDs. Both forms are kept as printed in their own builds; the discrepancy is flagged here and in the definitions for the wiring step.
- Name forms: Table 1 prints "Petrodomus tetradactylus" (missing an r), "Dasyprocta primnolopha", "Hydrochoerus hydrochoeris" and "Otolemur garnetti"; the build uses *Petrodromus tetradactylus*, *Dasyprocta prymnolopha*, *Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris* and *Otolemur garnettii*, as in the source builds and Herculano-Houzel et al. (2015), so the species keys join. The snapshot keeps the printed forms. The Methods call the spiny rat *Proechimys guyannensis* and the short-tailed shrew *Blarina carolinensis*; the table's names (*cayennensis*, *brevicauda*, as in the source papers) govern. The Methods also misprint the squirrel as "Sciureus carolinensis" and the galago as "Otolemur Garnettii".
- Sample-size conflicts in the printed record (definitions file): the agouti row's values are Herculano-Houzel et al. (2011)'s three-animal averages, although this paper's Methods count two agouti collected here — `Number` is 3, the sample behind the values; the spiny rat row is one animal (two bulbs pooled) although the 2011 build's `Number` counts 2.
- The snapshot was checked by the assistant against the PDF text and layout; human visual signoff remains pending in the registry. These are source measurements, not a normal-versus-condition contrast. No merge is performed by this build.
