# Sherwood 2003 dissertation Table 5-1: identified M1 cases

Source: Chester Charles Sherwood, *Neural substrates of primate communication: a comparative histometric study of the orofacial motor system* (Columbia University dissertation, 2003; UMI 3088421), Chapter 5 Table 5-1, printed p. 210 / local PDF p. 235. The 12 source rows were transcribed from the rendered page in source order and visually checked on 2026-11-25. The OCR misreads `12774` and `Cris`; the page image governs. Human visual signoff remains pending.

From the repository root, run `Rscript Sherwood__2003/Sherwood__2003_Table5-1.R`. The script reads only `Sherwood__2003_Table5-1_snapshot.csv` and writes the analysis CSV and `__Public/comparative-data/UMI3088421_Table5-1.tsv`. The definitions are in `reference_tables/`. No figure replot applies: this is a printed table.

This item is **specimen metadata only**. The source's ID, sex, age at death, fixation, and postmortem interval are retained as printed. Age is in years; `<10` is a strict upper bound, not an exact age. A source dash for perfused animals is an unreported postmortem interval, not zero hours. `Pongo pygmaeus` is the historical source label; it is not assigned to a modern orangutan species. Names and codes are study IDs as printed, not proven collection accessions. No brain or cell measurements, group means, or dissertation-only metrics are taken from Chapters 5–6 here.

The peer-reviewed Sherwood et al. (2004) **I** paper, *Brain, Behavior and Evolution* 63:61–81, Table 1 (printed p. 63), gives the same 12 rows in the same order with identical species, sex, age, fixation, and postmortem interval, but omits the IDs. Therefore the dissertation supplies the ID bridge; prefer the journal article for shared metadata and all published measurements. Chapter 6 Methods (printed p. 239 / PDF p. 264) explicitly refers back to Chapter 5 for acquisition and fixation of its 12 catarrhine M1 cases, so this roster also gives cohort context for the corresponding chemoarchitecture study. The exact individual-to-journal-row bridge rests on matching roster order and five demographic/preparation fields, not a journal-printed accession. Do not count the dissertation and journal as independent specimens.

The Chapter 5/6 sample is from the **right hemisphere orofacial primary motor cortex** (the right-side sampling is stated in the article Methods and Chapter 6). The source table itself does not print collection accession numbers or individual histometric values. A correction to the 2004 chemoarchitecture paper affects some reported layer-wise cell densities; this identity item does not endorse dissertation measurements in place of the corrected journal values.
