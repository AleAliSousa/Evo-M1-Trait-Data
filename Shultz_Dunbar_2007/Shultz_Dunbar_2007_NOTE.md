# Shultz & Dunbar 2007 — documented skip (species data not published)

Shultz, S., & Dunbar, R. I. M. (2007). The evolution of the social brain: anthropoid primates contrast
with other vertebrates. *Proc. R. Soc. B*, 274(1624), 2429–2436. https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2007.0693
Local files: `Shultz_Dunbar_2007.pdf`; ESM `rspb20070693supp1.doc`. Re-inspected 2026-10-28 by an AI
assistant (Claude Science): article text read; ESM converted to text (EndNote field codes stripped)
and its three embedded PNG figures extracted and viewed. Confirms the 2026-10-21 finding in `README.md`.

## Why no dataset item

| locus | content | why not built |
|---|---|---|
| Table 1 (article) | univariate results using phylogenetic brain-size residuals | statistics only |
| Table 2 (article) | minimum adequate models per taxon | statistics only |
| ESM text | data-source paragraph, best-model F statistics, per-species list of activity-budget sources | sources, no values |
| ESM Table S1 | definitions of diet, habitat, strata and activity categories per taxon | category definitions, no species assignments |
| ESM Figs. S1–S3 | phylogenies of primates (43 tip labels on Fig. S1, listed below), ungulates and carnivores with abbreviated tip labels | trees only; captions mention "phylogenetic residuals" / "social data" but no values are drawn |

The article directs readers to the ESM for "Information on the species included" (§2a) and "for
species data" (Discussion), but the ESM contains no species values — recorded as found, not inferred. Per-species brain sizes, group sizes, social-system
codes and activity budgets are therefore unavailable from this paper. Nothing was invented.

## What the paper compiles (roadmap)

`reference_tables/Shultz_Dunbar_2007_references.csv` — 37 rows: every work the ESM names as a data
source (all 34 entries of the ESM reference list, verbatim), plus three cited-but-absent sources
("Lan and Dunbar", "Dunbar unpub. Data", "R.Dunbar, unpublished"), each with the variable it supplies
and, for activity budgets, the species it covers exactly as printed (misspellings such as
"Callecebus", "Allouatta", "Siamiri", "Damaslicus" kept). Key primary sources: Stephan et al. 1981
(primate brain), Dunbar 1992 and Smuts et al. 1987 (primate group size), Dunbar & Bever 1998 and
Kruska 1988 (carnivore brain), Gittleman 1989 (carnivore sociality), Jerison 1973 / Oboussier 1966 /
Haarmann 1975 / Mangold-Wirz 1966 (ungulate brain). Data role if ever built: secondary (plus the
authors' own social-system classification).
