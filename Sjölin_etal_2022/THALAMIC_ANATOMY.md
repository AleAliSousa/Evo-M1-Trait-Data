# Thalamic anatomy — Sjölin et al. 2022

The source measures a **postmortem tissue sample labelled thalamus**. The sampled nucleus, coordinates, hemisphere, and dissection boundaries are **not specified in the inspected main article or supplementary Methods**. It is not established as a homogenate representing the whole thalamus.

## Primary evidence

- `Sjolin-2022-Distribution of five clinically im.pdf`, PDF p. 2, Tissue collection and Protein extraction: tissue from 17 selected CNS regions in 10 donors; samples were homogenised for ELISA.
- PDF p. 3, Table 2: one Thalamus entry under Diencephalon, with no nuclear subdivision.
- PDF p. 4, Fig. 1 and caption: region 12 is labelled thalamus. This is a schematic display of concentrations, not a dissection map defining which nuclei were physically sampled.
- `13041_2022_935_MOESM1_ESM.docx`, Supplementary Methods section 1, Preparation of brain samples: tissue was supplied in 2-mL tubes and approximately 450–550 mg was cut for extraction. No thalamus-specific sampling location is supplied. The later supplement tables repeat the broad regional label.
- The source supplementary data and compiled CSV retain the regional label and donor-level results; they do not resolve nuclear anatomy.

## Consequence for matching

Record the anatomical scope as **thalamic tissue, subregion unspecified**. Do not describe it as whole-thalamus tissue or assign it to mediodorsal thalamus from the label or schematic. Its high MBP concentration is not evidence identifying a particular nucleus or dissection boundary.

Neither Heiss's medial-thalamic VOI nor an unweighted mean of medial, lateral-geniculate and medial-geniculate measurements has a demonstrated anatomical match to this sample. Resolve with donor-dissection records or author clarification before treating a nuclear pairing as primary. No author was contacted during this review.
