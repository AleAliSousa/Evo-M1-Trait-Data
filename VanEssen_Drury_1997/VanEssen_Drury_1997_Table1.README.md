# Van Essen & Drury 1997 — Table 1 and associated Results text

Van Essen, D. C., & Drury, H. A. (1997). Structural and functional analyses of human cerebral cortex using a surface-based atlas. *Journal of Neuroscience, 17*(18), 7079–7102. https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.17-18-07079.1997

The item contains per-hemisphere Table 1 areas for the Visible Man, plus the human V1 and estimated total visual-cortex values and the macaque comparison reported in Results. `VanEssen_Drury_1997_Table1_snapshot.xlsx` preserves Table 1; `VanEssen_Drury_1997_Table1_text_snapshot.csv` freezes the text values and their row-level provenance.

The Visible Man is one adult male cadaver digital atlas reconstructed from 1 mm section images. Areas are tile-area sums on the three-dimensional reconstruction, not flat-map areas, and the authors consider the total a slight underestimate. The V1 rows are secondary within this paper (Rademacher et al. 1993 extents mapped to the atlas). The macaque case 79-0 reconstruction is from Drury et al. 1996a and is retained as `Macaca sp.` because this paper identifies it only as macaque. The 72 cm² value is total right-hemisphere neocortex; buried cortex is reported only as 59%, so no absolute buried area is invented.

The live registry uses `VanEssen_Drury_1997_Table1`; the R script writes the DOI-coded public TSV from that row.

The Results text also cites historical human V1 ranges from Filiminoff (1932) and Stensaas et al. (1974). Those secondary ranges are captured separately in `VanEssen_Drury_1997_CitedV1Ranges`; the Kaskan workbook's midpoint values are not primary observations.
