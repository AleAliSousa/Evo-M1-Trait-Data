# Ziege et al. 2016: rabbit population group-size means

Importance of latrine communication in European rabbits shifts along a rural-to-urban gradient. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12898-016-0083-y

Table1 and Methods extracted from preserved PMC XML (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4908761/fullTextXML), retrieved 2026-10-23. Fifteen population means around Frankfurt, Germany, range from 1.5 to 10 individuals. Methods explicitly describe behavioral observations augmented by ferrets driving social-group members out of burrows. The population rows retain coordinates, urbanity, density, age uncertainty and original source citation.

This is a same-originating-team re-report of the 2015 census study (https://doi.org/10.1111/jzo.12207), not an independent 2016 replicate. The 2015 full text was not retrieved; preserve that limitation. Marked primary at the team/data-origin level, with both DOI links; do not double-count when the earlier paper is added. Not found in local EndNote metadata search.

The merge summary is 4.14, the equal-weight mean of the 15 published site means. It is NOT a group-weighted mean, an adult-only mean, or a universal species mean. Underlying group sample sizes are unavailable here. The source-specific label states population mean and the definition states the aggregation. All population rows remain intact for later selection.
