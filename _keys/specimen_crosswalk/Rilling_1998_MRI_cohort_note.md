# Rilling 1998 MRI cohort and later paper identity

Rilling's Emory dissertation is dated **1998**, not 1996. Table 2-1 (PDF p. 95) prints 44 living MRI subject labels, sex, scan date, birth date, age and body weight. It is the earliest found source here that names this 44-subject roster. `_keys/specimen_crosswalk/specimen_source_registry.csv` records the source as `PUB_RILLING_DISSERTATION_1998`; 44 public source-label rows were added to `specimen_crosswalk.csv` under `source_publication=Rilling__1998`.

`Rilling__1998/reference_tables/Rilling__1998_Table2-1_identity_links.csv` is the row-level audit. MacLeod et al. 2003 Table 1 provides the individual Yerkes MRI labels and brain volumes. The dissertation has 36 direct name/code matches, seven probable matches and one thesis-only code (`RJe-1`). Six human H-codes are probable links to MacLeod's numbered human rows via unique sex and the dissertation Table 5-1 brain-volume sequence, not explicit shared IDs. `Bosanjo` versus `Bosandjo` is a probable spelling match. `Sunny (734 I)` is linked by name to MacLeod's Sunny; the printed `734 I` is not treated as Schenker's `736_IL`.

Rilling & Insel 1998 and 1999 Table 1 each describe a 44-subject, 11-species MRI sample but do not publish individual identifiers. Their relationship to the dissertation roster is retained as a **cohort-level candidate** for each subject, never as an individually confirmed article measurement. `_Table2-1_cohort_comparison.csv` records the published sex-count and mean differences. In particular, Table 2-1 has 27M/17F while its methods text says 26M/18F; both article tables say rhesus 2M/2F versus Table 2-1's 3M/1F. The 1998 article's gorilla and mangabey means differ from the 1999 values.

The four orangutans retain the source's historical *Pongo pygmaeus* concept. None has been reassigned to Bornean or Sumatran orangutan from this roster. The crosswalk rows are based entirely on published dissertation and MacLeod evidence. They add provenance, not a new independent sample to any merge.
