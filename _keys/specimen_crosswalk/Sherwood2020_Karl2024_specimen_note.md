# Specimen note: Sherwood 2020 and Karl 2024 V1 electron microscopy

## Purpose

Track potential reuse of biological specimens between Sherwood et al. (2020), *Cerebral Cortex* 30:5604–5615, doi:10.1093/cercor/bhaa149, and Karl et al. (2024), *Journal of Comparative Neurology*, doi:10.1002/cne.25669. Neither paper prints a specimen accession for the five primates considered here. All assignments below are public-evidence candidates.

## Evidence chain

1. Sherwood 2020 Supplementary Table 1 lists 27 specimens. The main paper reports a five-individual V1 electron-microscopy subset by species, sex, age, and hemisphere. Its 19-year-old male human and 11-year-old male baboon are probable same animals as corresponding Supplementary Table 1 rows; the sampled baboon hemisphere differs. The 16-year-old female lemur, 25-year-old male *Saimiri sciureus*, and 23-year-old female chimpanzee are additional to that table's individuals.
2. Karl 2024 Table 1 lists 18 mammals, including five primates. Its lemur (female, 16, right), *S. sciureus* (male, 25, right), and chimpanzee (female, 23, left) exactly match Sherwood's electron-microscopy descriptions on these four attributes. The authors and V1 electron-microscopy tissue context overlap. These are **probable** one-animal/two-paper links, pending shared accession or explicit author confirmation.
3. The Karl baboon is male, age 7, right; Sherwood's electron-microscopy baboon is male, age 11, right. Karl's 174.3 g brain mass equals Sherwood's Table 2 species mean, but this numerical agreement does not resolve the age conflict or establish individual identity.
4. Both papers describe a 19-year-old male human right-hemisphere sample. Sherwood states the El Paso County coroner supplied its human brains. Karl states NIH NeuroBioBank supplied its human brain. Karl's 1336 g brain mass is a literature-derived adult male average, not a mass measured from that donor. No cross-paper human identity is asserted.

## Registry treatment

Three Karl Table 1 rows share source-local canonical IDs with the probable Sherwood electron-microscopy specimens. The human and baboon are unlinked across papers. Sherwood's human and baboon electron-microscopy rows share canonical IDs with their probable within-paper Supplementary Table 1 counterparts. All are marked `probable` where identity is inferred without a printed accession. The other 15 Karl individuals were not ingested in this targeted comparison.

## Measurement boundary

Sherwood Supplementary Table 2 contains species means for layer-specific V1 and IT synapse and neuron densities per **mm³**. Karl Table 1 contains specimen-level full-depth V1 areal densities per **mm²**. These values are methodologically distinct and must not be merged as measurements of the same quantity. The Sherwood species mean for a two-individual species is not a specimen measurement. Probable shared specimens must not be counted as independent biological replicates across the two papers.

See `Sherwood_etal_2020/Sherwood2020_Karl2024_specimen_comparison.csv` for each primate's reported attributes and measurements, with units in column names.
